The Review Sites That Matter Most for Local Business Success

If you're running a local business, online reviews aren't just a nice-to-have — they're one of the most powerful forces driving customers through your door. Studies show that 93% of consumers read online reviews before making a purchase decision, and nearly half won't even consider a business with fewer than four stars. But here's the catch: not all review platforms carry the same weight. Spreading yourself too thin across every review site on the internet is a recipe for burnout and mediocre results. Instead, smart local business owners focus their energy on the platforms that actually move the needle. In this guide, we'll break down the review sites that matter most for local businesses — and show you how to make the most of each one.

1. Google Business Profile: The Undisputed King of Local Reviews

If you only focus on one review platform, make it Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business). When someone searches for your business — or any business like yours — Google reviews are the first thing they see. Your star rating appears directly in Google Search results and on Google Maps, which means your reputation is literally front and center before a potential customer even clicks on your website.

Google reviews also have a direct impact on your local SEO rankings. Businesses with more reviews, higher ratings, and recent activity tend to rank higher in the coveted "Local Pack" — the top three business listings that appear in local search results. More visibility means more clicks, more calls, and more foot traffic.

Key takeaway: Make getting Google reviews your top priority. Every new review strengthens your local search presence and builds immediate trust with potential customers.

2. Yelp: Still Essential for Certain Industries

Love it or hate it, Yelp remains one of the most influential review platforms for specific local business categories. If you're in restaurants, food service, home services, beauty and wellness, or retail, Yelp is still a major player. Millions of people open the Yelp app specifically to find local businesses, which means users on this platform often have high purchase intent.

Yelp also powers reviews on Apple Maps and Siri, which significantly extends its reach. A strong Yelp presence means your business is well-represented across the entire Apple ecosystem — a massive audience that's easy to overlook.

One important note: Yelp has a strict policy against directly asking customers for reviews. Focus on making it easy for satisfied customers to find your Yelp page, and let great service do the talking.

Key takeaway: Claim and optimize your Yelp listing, respond to all reviews professionally, and keep your business information current. The passive visibility alone is worth the effort.

3. Facebook: Where Trust and Community Overlap

With nearly three billion active users, Facebook is more than a social network — it's a trusted community where word-of-mouth referrals happen at scale. Facebook's review and recommendation system allows users to recommend (or not recommend) businesses, and these recommendations are visible to the reviewer's entire friend network.

This social proof element is uniquely powerful. When a potential customer sees that their friend or neighbor recommended your business on Facebook, that carries far more weight than an anonymous review from a stranger. For local businesses especially, community trust is everything.

Facebook reviews also appear in Google search results for your business name, giving them added SEO visibility. Keeping your Facebook page active and well-reviewed creates a consistent, trustworthy impression across multiple touchpoints.

Key takeaway: Encourage happy customers to leave a Facebook recommendation, and engage with your Facebook community regularly to build the kind of local loyalty that drives long-term growth.

4. Industry-Specific Review Platforms You Shouldn't Ignore

Beyond the big three, there are several niche review sites that carry enormous weight in specific industries. Depending on your business type, these platforms can be just as important — or even more important — than Google or Yelp:

  • TripAdvisor — Essential for restaurants, hotels, attractions, and tourism-related businesses. TripAdvisor ranks high in search results for travel-related queries and draws a highly motivated audience.
  • Houzz — The go-to platform for home improvement contractors, interior designers, and architects. A strong Houzz profile can generate high-quality leads from homeowners actively planning projects.
  • Healthgrades, Zocdoc & WebMD — Critical for healthcare providers, dentists, and medical practices. Patients increasingly rely on these platforms when choosing a doctor or specialist.
  • Avvo — Important for attorneys and legal professionals. Potential clients frequently check Avvo ratings before scheduling a consultation.
  • Angi (formerly Angie's List) — Highly valuable for plumbers, electricians, roofers, landscapers, and other home service professionals actively looking for vetted leads.

Key takeaway: Identify the one or two industry-specific platforms most relevant to your business and treat them with the same seriousness as Google. A strong niche presence can set you apart from competitors who only focus on general platforms.

5. The BBB and Nextdoor: Trust and Neighborhood Credibility

Two often-overlooked platforms can provide a meaningful credibility boost for local businesses: the Better Business Bureau (BBB) and Nextdoor.

A BBB accreditation or high BBB rating signals professionalism and trustworthiness — especially to older demographics and customers making significant purchasing decisions. BBB profiles also rank well in search engines, giving you another positive result on page one when someone Googles your business name.

Nextdoor, meanwhile, is hyper-local by design. Neighbors recommend businesses to neighbors, and a strong Nextdoor presence can generate a steady stream of referrals from people who live right in your service area. For businesses that depend on local repeat customers — like landscapers, cleaners, pet services, or local restaurants — Nextdoor recommendations can be pure gold.

Key takeaway: Don't overlook the credibility and community-building power of the BBB and Nextdoor. A few strong recommendations from real neighbors can have an outsized impact on your local reputation.

How to Build a Consistent Review Strategy That Actually Works

Knowing which platforms matter is only half the battle. The real challenge is consistently generating new reviews across the sites that matter most to your business. Here's a practical framework to get started:

  1. Prioritize your platforms. Choose two or three review sites based on your industry and audience. Don't try to win everywhere at once.
  2. Make it easy to leave a review. Send follow-up emails or text messages with direct links to your review pages. The fewer clicks it takes, the more reviews you'll receive.
  3. Ask at the right moment. The best time to request a review is right after a positive experience — when satisfaction is highest and the interaction is fresh in the customer's mind.
  4. Respond to every review. Responding to both positive and negative reviews shows that you're engaged, professional, and genuinely care about your customers. This matters to potential customers reading those reviews later.
  5. Monitor your reputation consistently. Set up alerts or use a reputation management tool so you never miss a new review. Staying on top of your online presence lets you address issues quickly and celebrate wins when they happen.
